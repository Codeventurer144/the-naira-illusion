How the dashboard was built

Methodology

This page explains where the data came from, what was cleaned or transformed, how each measure is calculated, and where the model has limits.

01
Dataset

132 monthly observations from January 2014 to December 2024

The production dataset combines three historical series: headline consumer prices from the National Bureau of Statistics, deposit rates from the Central Bank of Nigeria, and the CBN official/reference NGN-per-USD exchange rate. The monthly table contains CPI, the savings rate, 3-month, 6-month and 12-month deposit rates, and USD/NGN.

02
Data preparation

Cleaning and transformation before the dashboard

The source tables were brought to a common monthly format, checked for duplicate months and missing core fields, and aligned to the same January 2014–December 2024 period. CPI stays on the pre-2025 NBS basis so the series is internally comparable instead of mixing two different CPI systems.

  • Dates were standardised to one monthly YYYY-MM key so CPI, deposit rates and exchange rates could be joined consistently.
  • Isolated gaps in the historical CPI table were reconstructed from published year-on-year NBS inflation using CPIₜ = CPIₜ₋₁₂ × (1 + YoY / 100). This is algebraic reconstruction, not interpolation.
  • For the later exchange-rate period, monthly values were derived from the CBN official/reference daily central rate where the monthly series was not directly available.
  • Missing fixed-deposit rates were deliberately left blank. The simulator stops when a required rollover rate is missing instead of inventing a number.
  • The 2025 rebased CPI series was not appended to this historical module because doing so would mix incompatible CPI bases.
03
Savings calculations

How the account balance is simulated

For the ordinary savings option, the published annual savings rate is converted to a monthly rate and compounded month by month. Fixed deposits are treated differently because a 3-, 6- or 12-month rate is intended to stay fixed for that term.

Savings account Balanceₜ = Balanceₜ₋₁ × (1 + annual rate / 1200)
Fixed deposit at maturity Maturity factor = 1 + (rate / 100) × (months / 12)

At the end of a fixed-deposit term, principal plus interest is rolled into the next term using the rate available in that rollover month.

04
Purchasing power

Turning the bank balance into a real-value measure

A larger naira balance does not necessarily mean the saver can buy more. The dashboard therefore expresses the end balance in the purchasing power of the selected starting month.

Inflation-adjusted balance Real balance = Nominal balance × CPI at start / CPI now
Real return Real return = Real balance / Starting balance − 1

If the real return is negative, the account can still have earned interest while losing purchasing power.

05
Exchange-rate view

Showing the same balance in official-rate US dollars

The USD view is another way to show how the external value of the naira changed. The dashboard divides the simulated naira balance by the CBN monthly official/reference NGN-per-USD rate. It does not use parallel-market exchange rates.

USD equivalent USD value = Nominal naira balance / NGN per USD
USD wealth index USD index = Current USD value / Starting USD value × 100

07
Sources and limitations

What the dashboard does not try to reproduce

This is a historical deposit-rate simulation, not a statement from a particular Nigerian bank. Actual customer returns can differ because banks have product-specific rules, fees, taxes, minimum balances, rate tiers, early-withdrawal penalties and different compounding conventions.

The project also uses the official/reference exchange-rate series for consistency. It does not claim that a saver could always obtain US dollars at that rate.

i
In simple terms

The dashboard asks one practical question: after your account earns interest, can the money actually buy more than it could when you started? It answers that by comparing the simulated account balance with inflation and the official exchange rate over the same period.
